# B699 precedent follow-up

Primary source read2026-09-08: Silvia Casacuberta, On the divisibility of binomial coefficients, arXiv1906.07652v1 (2019), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.07652 .

Theorem2.5 uses a prime power dividing n and a prime near n/d to restrict possible binomial indices to a union of small residue intervals. Corollary2.6 gives the familiar row-cover sufficient condition from a prime q near n and a large prime power divisor of n. The mathematical setting originates with Shareshian and Woodroofe (2018). These are closely related to our low-residue/CRT mechanism; they do not automatically prove the exact two-offset cofactor formulation recorded in explorer/constraints.md, and that correspondence has not been fully audited.

The a=0,b=1 special case has a particularly direct CRT interpretation: if P|n, Q|n-1 are eligible prime powers and PQ>n-1, the congruences j=0 modP and j in{0,1} modQ leave no interior row index. This observation reinforces the need to distinguish a useful reconstruction/generalization from a novelty claim.

The primary also located Schur's odd-product theorem as quoted in Safwan Akbik (1991), DOI10.1155/S0161171291000947. It suggests a central j=n/2 exclusion fori>=6 after matching h=j-floor(i/2), k=floor(i/2). The exact original theorem was not fully audited and no new central-case theorem is accepted from this lead. The denominator-two bridge is proved on paper in explorer/constraints.md. Keep this branch as an explicitly uncompleted source match.
