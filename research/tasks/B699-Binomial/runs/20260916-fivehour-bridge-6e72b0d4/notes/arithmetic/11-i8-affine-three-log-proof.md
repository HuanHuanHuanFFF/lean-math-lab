# i=8 and 9: a full two-exponent family by three logarithms and affine lattices

Author `/root/arithmetic`. Completed author paper proof and two exact finite implementations; parent independent acceptance is separate. No Lean. This result does not invoke a historical position consumer. It uses the six-window integer invariant, Yu's explicit three-logarithm estimate, and exact two-dimensional affine lattice enumeration.

## 1. Frontier change and exact scope

For every pair of integers A,B>=2, put `n=6*5^A*7^B`. Then for each i in {8,9} and every integer j with `i<j<=floor(n/2)`, a prime p>=i divides both C(n,i) and C(n,j).

This closes a genuine unrestricted two-exponent family, including all parities of A and B. It is not the whole i=8 or i=9 problem, and it is not a theorem for arbitrary 2357-smooth n or arbitrary shifts. No claim of historical novelty for every input in this family is made. The reusable new interface is a fixed third logarithm handled by an **affine**, rather than homogeneous, two-dimensional lattice.

The minimum n is 7350, so all six-window positivity and j,k boundary assumptions hold. For these indices the small part is `s(x)=2^v2(x)*3^v3(x)*5^v5(x)*7^v7(x)`. Complete powers of every larger prime are retained. Under the negation of the original common-prime statement, the six-window integer invariant gives

`1 <= T6^4*(3n^2-20n+24) / [2^18*27*(n-1)^4*(n-3)^3*(n-5)^2]`,

where `T6=product(s(n-r),r=0,...,5)`. This is the same polynomial identity and source-divisibility argument reconstructed in 08 and `sixg_identity_verify.py`: source primes are at least 11, exceeding the degree 6 and both target factorial denominators. The invariant is applied to the same n,j. It is not an assertion that NC8 implies NC7. For i=9 one may either apply the identical source argument or transfer the i=8 witness by `C(n,9)=C(n,8)*(n-8)/9`, since that witness is at least 11.

## 2. Exact denominator mass yields a linear valuation requirement

Write P=5^A*7^B, H=A+B, and

`C2=v2(n-2)=1+v2(3P-1)`,

`C3=v3(n-3)=1+v3(2P-1)`,

`Q=2^C2*3^C3`, `C=max(C2,C3)`.

Since A,B>=2, the six small parts are exactly

`[n, 1, 2^C2, 3^C3, 2, 5]`.

Thus **T6=10nQ**. In particular the fifth offset has exactly one 5-factor; higher complete 5-powers have not been discarded. Since n-r>n/2 for r<=5, the displayed SIXG upper bound is strictly less than

`(30000/(512*27))*Q^4/n^3 < 3Q^4/n^3`.

Any remaining NC input therefore satisfies `n^3<3Q^4`. Using Q<=6^C, 6^4<5^5, 3<5, and C>=1,

`5^(3H)<n^3<3*6^(4C)<5^(1+5C)<=5^(6C)`.

Consequently **H<2C**. Choose q in {2,3} attaining C and put v=C-1. With c=3 for q=2 and c=2 for q=3, this gives

`v=v_q(c*5^A*7^B-1)`, **H<2(v+1)**.

For H>=8 the relevant v is at least 4. All H<8 are retained as a separate finite bottom set.

## 3. A fully explicit global exponent cap from Yu

Primary source: Kunrui Yu, *Linear forms in p-adic logarithms III*, Compositio Mathematica **91** (1994), 241--276, **section 0.1, printed pages 241--242**. [NUMDAM primary PDF](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1994__91_3_241_0.pdf).

The downloaded exact bytes are at `.tools/replay/6e72b0d4-arithmetic/yu-1994.pdf`, 1,762,322 bytes, SHA256 `caea575fec7e5abb1fc7c4397cbd7a5f0f76e2b81a30aeb275b7c60ad8a1d846`. Author visually checked `yu-main-02.png` and `yu-main-03.png`. These are temporary local source copies, not a new tracked redistribution. Parent source review is separately recorded.

The explicit consequence stated there is, with ell algebraic numbers, their generated field degree d, residue degree f, and sorted admissible heights h_j,

`Xi < Phi*log(d*Bmax)`,

`Phi=22000*(9.5*(ell+1)*d/sqrt(log q))^(2*(ell+1))`

`     *(q^f-1)*h1*...*h_ell*log(10*ell*d*h')`,

where `Bmax=max(|b1|,...,|b_ell|,3)`, `h'=max(h_ell,1)`, and each

`h_j>=max(h(alpha_j), |log alpha_j|/(10d), log q)`.

Use precisely the first displayed estimate, with h'=h_ell; no optional replacement or extra condition on b_ell is used. In this application:

| parameter | q=2 | q=3 |
|---|---|---|
| alpha tuple | (5,7,3) | (5,7,2) |
| exponent tuple | (A,B,1) | (A,B,1) |
| field / degree / ramification / residue degree | Q / 1 / 1 / 1 | Q / 1 / 1 / 1 |
| ell | 3 | 3 |
| h1,h2,h3,h' | 10,10,10,10 | 10,10,10,10 |
| Bmax | max(A,B,3)<=H | max(A,B,3)<=H |

All rational numbers are positive, nonzero q-adic units; their rational logarithmic heights and real logarithms are below 10. The form is a positive integer minus one, hence nonzero. The section 0.1 estimate is stated for the field generated by the given numbers; no unmentioned root-of-unity extension is used. Neither multiplicative independence nor a principal-unit restriction is added to the source statement.

The deliberately loose numerical bound is

`Phi < 10^5 * 10^16 * 10 * 10^3 * 10 = 10^26`.

Indeed log q>1/2 gives `38/sqrt(log q)<76<100`; the power is 8, q-1<10, and log(300)<10. Hence

`v<10^26*log H`, `H<2+2*10^26*log H`.

This implies **H<10^32**. For H0=10^32, log H0=32 log10<96 makes the right side smaller than H0. The function `H-2-2*10^26*log H` has positive derivative for H>=H0. This global cap is proved before any valuation search; termination is not inferred from sampled growth of p-adic roots.

## 4. The two affine congruence towers

Squaring the exact source congruence gives the necessary condition

`25^A*49^B=c^(-2) modulo q^v`.

For q=2 begin at v=3 with M=2^(v-3); for q=3 begin at v=1 with M=3^(v-1). The valuations `v2(25-1)=3` and `v3(25-1)=1`, together with elementary LTE, show that 25 has exact order M in the respective principal-unit group. Both 49 and c^(-2) belong to that group. There are unique residues L_v,T_v in [0,M) satisfying

`25^L_v=49`, `25^T_v=c^(-2) modulo q^v`.

Every next residue lies in the full set of q lifts `L+zM` or `T+zM`, z=0,...,q-1. The generator checks all of them. Thus the necessary congruence is

**A+L_v*B=T_v modulo M**.

Its full solution set is the translate `(T_v,0)+Lambda_v`, where Lambda_v has basis `(M,0),(-L_v,1)`. Stored reduced bases b1,b2 are certified to be full lattice bases by their congruences and absolute determinant M. Their exact Gauss reduction inequalities are also checked, although the coverage argument only needs the full basis property.

### An exact global stopping test

For any desired square `low<=A,B<=high`, write `(A,B)=(T,0)+m*b1+k*b2`. With determinant Delta, the unique real coordinates are

`m=[b2_y*(A-T)-b2_x*B]/Delta`,

`k=[-b1_y*(A-T)+b1_x*B]/Delta`.

Normalize Delta>0. The minimum and maximum of each linear numerator over the square occur at its corners. Taking integer ceilings and floors yields a complete integer rectangle for m,k. Enumerate that rectangle and retain the actual points in the square with A+B below the desired cap. This is exact bounded enumeration, with no floating nearest-point or LLL acceptance assumption.

At every prospective stopping level use `low=2, high=10^32-1` and `A+B<10^32`. A squared-congruence point may have the wrong sign, or at q=2 a different square root of 1. Therefore each remaining point is tested in the **original** source congruence modulo q^v. If none passes, this entire global exponent triangle contains no source solution of valuation **at least v**. All higher levels are excluded as subsets. A root platform cannot invalidate this stop.

The exact stops are:

| q | stop v | full coordinate rectangle size | points in global triangle | original-source solutions |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 212 | 21 | 4 | 0 |
| 3 | 135 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The four q=2 points have 32-digit exponents; modular powering checks them without constructing 5^A or 7^B. Their full coordinates, basis and nonzero remainders are in the certificate. The q=3 inverse-coordinate rectangle is already empty.

### Finite levels below the stop

If the selected source has exact valuation v below the stop, the linear requirement is `A+B<=2v+1`. Enumerate all affine-lattice points A,B>=2 in that triangle, retain H>=8, and test exact source valuation using its residue modulo q^(v+1): nonzero but divisible by q^v. Thus exact-v candidates and at-least-v stopping are kept distinct.

There are three high candidates, all from q=2:

`(A,B,v)=(7,3,5), (3,5,5), (3,9,8)`.

The bottom H<8 has ten pairs. All **13** resulting pairs satisfy the strict SIXG inequality, using their full small parts. No finite original-prime certificate or probable-prime assertion is required at this endpoint.

## 5. Reproducible evidence and limits

Its only imported helper is the frozen exact Gauss algorithm from `i6_two_high_lattice_probe.py`. The output `i8_5_7_affine_probe.json` is a 172,518-byte exact integer certificate.

`i8_5_7_affine_verify.py` imports neither generator nor lattice/CAS package. It rechecks every modulus, exact group order, log residue, full lift relation, basis determinant/congruence and reduction inequality. At finite levels it independently scans **residue progressions in B**, rather than coordinate boxes. At the final stops it derives coordinate bounds by signed linear intervals rather than four-corner enumeration, reconstructs all global points, and checks their original-source remainders. Finally it rebuilds all six small parts and strict SIXG margins for the 13 pairs.
