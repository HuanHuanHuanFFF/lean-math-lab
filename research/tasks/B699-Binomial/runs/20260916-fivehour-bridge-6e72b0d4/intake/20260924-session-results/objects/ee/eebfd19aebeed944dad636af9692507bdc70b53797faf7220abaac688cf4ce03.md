## 1. A degree-loss specialization inequality

Let F be a primitive nonzero polynomial in Z[N,X], with deg_X F<=h.
Select r irreducible factors of F over Q[N,X], allowing repeated copies up to their
multiplicities, each with X-degree e_i>=m>=1. Normalize each factor and the leftover
factor to primitive integer polynomials. Gauss's content argument gives

    F = +/- A_1 ... A_r R.

For completeness, a product of primitive multivariate integer polynomials is
primitive: for each prime ell its reductions are nonzero, and F_ell[N,X] is an
integral domain, so their product is nonzero. Clearing rational factors and using
this content statement yields the displayed factorization up to a unit.

Let K be any finite field of characteristic ell, and let a be in K. Suppose

    f(X) = F(a,X) mod ell

is nonzero. Then every factor in the displayed product specializes to a nonzero
polynomial. Set d_i=deg_X(A_i(a,X) mod ell). We have 0<=d_i<=e_i.
The analogous degree inequality holds for R. Thus

    h-deg f >= sum_i(e_i-d_i).

Each selected factor which specializes to a constant uses at least m of this
lost-degree budget. Their number is at most floor((h-deg f)/m).
Each remaining selected factor has positive degree after specialization and
contributes at least one irreducible factor of f, counted WITH multiplicity.
If Omega_K(f) is that multiplicity-counted number, then

    r <= Omega_K(f) + floor((h-deg f)/m).                 (DL)

No monic-mod-ell assumption is made. Rational denominators and leading coefficient
loss are included by first passing to a primitive integer F and retaining the
lost-degree term. No squarefreeness of F or of its specialization is assumed.
A zero specialization gives no bound and must not be used.

This round uses m=3. Inequality (DL) bounds all Q-irreducible factors of X-degree
at least 3, including nonloadable ones and including multiplicity; it therefore
bounds the number of different loadable factors in C_G.

## 2. Why a complete finite projective cover controls all rational parameters

Let A be any homogeneous integer linear system for the coefficients of polynomials
of weight <=2h. Let W_ell=ker(A mod ell), of dimension d over F_ell, and let
B_1,...,B_d be a COMPLETE basis of this kernel.

For any nonzero rational solution F, clear denominators and divide the integer
content. Its primitive reduction is nonzero and belongs to W_ell. It is therefore
alpha * sum c_i B_i, where alpha is nonzero and the first nonzero c_i is 1.
These normalized vectors are precisely P^(d-1)(F_ell), with cardinality

    1+ell+...+ell^(d-1).

Suppose that for every such vector c, a certificate chooses a finite extension K
of F_ell and an a in K, and proves that the specialization is nonzero and the
right side of (DL) is <=7. Scaling by alpha changes neither degree nor Omega.
Then EVERY rational solution of A has at most seven irreducible factors of
X-degree >=3. This is a universal exclusion, not an inference that a finite-field
solution lifts to Q. All projective directions, including those with zero reduced
X^h coefficient, are included.

The number a is an auxiliary algebraic specialization coordinate. It is not a
replacement for the original natural number n or for the original NC9 point.

## 4. Complete modular kernels and reconstructed coefficients

The F_ell[N]-module interpolation algorithm is inherited as a mathematical method
from the H109 proof, whose direct invariant is reproduced here in compressed form.
For each local fixed j, process i in increasing order. On the current kernel,

    L(a(N)F)=a(r)L(F),

because every positive power of N-r multiplies an already imposed predecessor jet.
For current basis B_b and discrepancies d_b=L(B_b), choose a nonzero discrepancy
with minimum leading pair (weighted degree, X-position), indexed by a. Replace

    B_b <- B_b-(d_b/d_a)B_a  (b!=a),
    B_a <- (N-r)B_a.

Every new vector satisfies the new condition. Conversely, rewriting a kernel
combination in the new vectors leaves a coefficient of B_a divisible by N-r,
so these vectors generate the entire new kernel. Their determinant is a nonzero
multiple of N-r, so they remain a module basis. Distinct leading X-positions are
preserved. The predictable leading term gives the exact bounded dimension

    nu=sum_b max(0,2h-w_b+1).

The proof does not require ell>2h; Hasse jets and division only by nonzero field
discrepancies are used. Forward and reverse source orders are fully regenerated.
For these six systems all bounded basis vectors have weight exactly 2h, so no
N-multiple has to be added. The generator explicitly rejects a w_b<2h case:
applying it elsewhere requires expanding all N^k B_b with k+w_b<=2h.

Unlike the H109 output, this round reconstructs the COMPLETE modular coefficients.
Store the update constants c_b=d_b/d_a. For a target terminal basis vector, pull
its coefficient vector backwards through the transposed updates:

    f_a_old=(N-r)f_a_new-sum_(b!=a)c_b f_b_new,
    f_b_old=f_b_new (b!=a).

The initial module basis is X^b, hence the final f_b(N) are full coefficients.
The bound on their degrees is checked. Their five specialization vectors are
also compared with forward functional propagation.

A separate receiver independently expands these full coefficients at all original
sources, checks every jet and their linear independence. Forward/reverse full
coefficient bases have identical row-reduced spans. Completeness is justified by
the module invariant and exact terminal weight calculation, not by merely finding
several independent solutions. All implementations are by the same author/session;
this is not external peer review.

## 5. Factor-degree certificates, including multiplicities

For a monic polynomial B over F_q and a positive d, the following certify that B
is a squarefree product of irreducible polynomials all of degree d:

    X^(q^d) = X mod B,
    gcd(B, X^(q^(d/r))-X)=1 for every prime divisor r of d.

The first identity makes every factor squarefree and of degree dividing d. Any
proper divisor of d divides d/r for some prime r|d; the gcd tests exclude it.
Consequently Omega(B)=deg(B)/d. This is the usual Frobenius/Rabin criterion,
applied to an equal-degree block rather than only to one irreducible factor.

Each certificate contains a nonzero scalar and blocks (B,d,m), and verifies

    f=scalar * product B^m.

Thus Omega(f)=sum m*deg(B)/d. Repeated factors and overlaps do not invalidate the
multiplicity count; squarefreeness of f is not required.

The discovery program uses exact squarefree and distinct-degree decomposition.
The independent prime-field receiver does not trust that decomposition algorithm:
it checks the product and the Frobenius/gcd identities. It forms the F_11-linear
Frobenius operator in F_11[X]/(B) and applies it with bounded exact integer sums.
No floating point arithmetic or rank thresholds occur.

There are 83,250 certified equal-degree blocks, not 83,250 necessarily distinct
irreducible factors. Their product and degree/multiplicity information are retained.

## 8. Mandatory negative controls and scope

The receiver rejects a changed basis coefficient, a changed factor block, and
an omitted projective direction. A primitive factor such as 11X^3+1 can reduce to
a constant, so the degree-loss term cannot be omitted. Two different characteristic-
zero factors may coincide modulo 11, so factors of f must be counted with multiplicity.
Zero specializations are never accepted. These are method counterexamples, not
B699 counterexamples.

Only modular bases have been recovered. The giant rational bases and the actual
G factorization remain unrecovered. The six positive-dimensional rational source
spaces remain mathematically nonempty; their eight-loadable-factor loci are empty.
